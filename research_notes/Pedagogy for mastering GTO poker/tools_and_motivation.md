## 1. What modes do the main poker study products offer, and how do they score answers when the solution is a mixed frequency?

### Takeaway
The market has converged on three mode families: browse a solved strategy (study), play against the solution and be graded (practice/drill), and upload real hands to be graded (analyze); aggregated flop reports and node-locking sit on top for pattern-finding and exploit study. Grading of mixed strategies falls into three camps: pure EV loss (any action the equilibrium mixes is roughly free), frequency-weighted credit (partial points in proportion to solver frequency, optionally with a random-number roll that names one "best" action), and frequency-threshold verdicts; products disagree and users report confusion when two equal-EV actions get different labels.

### Cited Findings

**GTO Wizard (market leader)**
- [PRODUCT] The Trainer's primary metric is the "GTOW Score"; session pages also show total EV loss in big blinds versus GTO play and average EV loss per hand. — [GTO Wizard help: Measure Performance](https://help.gtowizard.com/measure-performance/)
- [PRODUCT] Move tiers include "Inaccuracy: moves taken less than 3.5% of the time in GTO that don't lose significant EV" and "Wrong Move: moves that are not taken in GTO". — [GTO Wizard help: Measure Performance](https://help.gtowizard.com/measure-performance/)
- [PRODUCT] "Best" versus "correct" is separated by frequency: with RNG mode off the best action is the highest-frequency action; with RNG mode on it is the action the random roll selects. "Making the best actions will net you maximum points, while lower frequency actions are scored according to their percentages. Blunders lose points according to their loss in percentage pot." — [GTO Wizard help: How To Use the Trainer](https://help.gtowizard.com/how-to-use-the-trainer/)
- [PRODUCT] A separate "Frequency Difference" stat "measures the sum of frequencies that you missed compared to the most frequent action in every spot"; a high value means the learner is taking low-frequency lines. The score formula was later revised to add EV loss as a percentage of pot and frequency difference. — [GTO Wizard help: Measure Performance](https://help.gtowizard.com/measure-performance/)
- [PRODUCT] A "frequency input" mode lets the learner enter the mix itself rather than one action; the help page recommends it for mixed preflop decisions. — [GTO Wizard help: How To Use the Trainer](https://help.gtowizard.com/how-to-use-the-trainer/)
- [PRODUCT] Study mode shows strategy matrices and a Reports tab; aggregate reports hold data from all 1,755 strategically distinct flops for a spot "to research deeper trends in the GTO strategy". — [GTO Wizard help: Study Mode](https://help.gtowizard.com/study-mode/)
- [PRODUCT] Custom aggregated reports (2025) let the user define a spot and get a snapshot of every flop node, filterable by texture; reported as an Elite-tier feature. — [PokerNews, June 2025](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2025/06/gto-wizard-customizable-aggregated-reports-48758.htm); [GTO Wizard blog: Introducing Custom Aggregated Reports](https://blog.gtowizard.com/introducing_custom_aggregated_reports/)
- [PRODUCT] Node-locking fixes one player's strategy at a decision point so the solver computes the best response, used to build exploitative strategies. — [GTO Wizard blog: Introducing Nodelocking](https://blog.gtowizard.com/introducing-nodelocking/)
- [PRACTITIONER, third-party review] After each trainer action the tool reports whether the play matched the solver, the optimal play, and EV gained or lost; uploaded real hands are checked against a GTO score. — [VIP-Grinders GTO Wizard review 2026](https://www.vip-grinders.com/poker-tools/gto-wizard-review/)

**Other trainers and their grading rules**
- [PRODUCT] Lucid Poker rates each decision Best / Good / Inaccuracy / Mistake / Blunder, gives a 0-100 grade per drill session, a breakdown of ratings and a "biggest blunder" callout with its EV cost. (Search engine flagged the page as roughly two years old.) — [Lucid Poker](https://lucidpoker.com/)
- [PRODUCT] Postflop+ grades Perfect / OK / Mistake / Blunder and keeps an Elo-style rating that starts at 1200 and moves with the difficulty and accuracy of decisions. — [Postflop+ product page](https://thinkgto.com/apps/postflop-plus)
- [COMMUNITY] A Postflop+ App Store reviewer: "If two actions have the same EV, why is one 'ok' and one 'perfect'?" The developer reply did not explain the rule. This is the clearest documented user confusion about frequency-based labels on indifferent hands. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1488850006)
- [PRODUCT] DriveGTO grades by EV only: it names the highest-EV action "GTO best" and computes EV loss as chosen EV minus best EV, on the reasoning that actions an equilibrium mixes have near-identical EV and small gaps are mostly solver convergence noise. — [DriveGTO manual](https://drivehud.com/dh2-knowledge-base/drivegto-manual-new/)
- [PRODUCT] Poker Academy's trainer labels best / correct / inaccuracy / mistake / blunder, with the grade derived from EV given up relative to the pot "not from a list of approved buttons". — [Poker Academy practice guide](https://poker.academy/blog/post/practice-full-hand-training-guide)
- [PRODUCT] NTPoker uses Excellent / Good / Not Good / Wrong / Blunder, where Excellent means no EV loss and Blunder a very large EV loss. — [NTPoker grade guide](https://ntpoker.jp/en/service/grade_management_guide)
- [PRODUCT] Other trainers grade by solver frequency thresholds (one describes "Solver plays your action at least 20% of the time" as a verdict boundary, with blunder meaning the solver essentially never takes the action with that exact hand); another offers three selectable grading modes, the most lenient counting any action the solver plays as correct. — [RangeConverter trainer guide](https://rangeconverter.com/articles/gto-poker-trainer-guide); [jarvispoker trainer](https://jarvispoker.com/trainer)
- [COMMUNITY] An open-source clone of GTO Wizard's practice mode estimates preflop EV loss from how far the hand sits from where the chart uses that action (an approximation, not the official method). — [GitHub p68/GTO-trainer](https://github.com/p68/GTO-trainer)
- [PRODUCT] DTO Poker is built on GTO solutions for MTT and SNG scenarios with adjustable stack depth; free tier plus "Grinder" and "High Roller" subscriptions; iOS rating about 4.7. One user complained of no videos or explanations of why an action was an error. — [App Store: DTO MTT](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1458491856); [AppFollow listing](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/dto-mtt-gto-poker-trainer/1458491856?country=us)
- [PRACTITIONER, vendor blog with self-interest] A 2026 comparison argues a good trainer should use "EV-loss grading that measures how much each mistake costs rather than flagging it right or wrong". — [GTO Gecko blog](https://gtogecko.com/blog/best-gto-apps-platforms)

**Market structure**
- [PRACTITIONER] Comparisons split the market into local engines run on the user's hardware (PioSOLVER, TexasSolver, GTO+) and browser libraries of precomputed solutions (GTO Wizard and similar). GTO Wizard is described as the choice for node-locking and custom solves, PioSOLVER as giving most control over tree building (listed at $249 Pro / $475 Edge one-time), DTO as a budget MTT drill tool, Lucid GTO as simple cash drills (listed at $49/month). Prices conflict between sources and should be rechecked. — [Sharkling comparison](https://sharkling.io/blog/best-poker-solver); [GTO LAB comparison](https://gtolab.com/poker-training-reviews/best-gto-solvers-tournament-poker-2026/); [GTO Gecko software roundup](https://gtogecko.com/blog/best-poker-software); [Pokerfuse solver guide](https://pokerfuse.com/learn-poker/tools/poker-solvers/)
- [PRACTITIONER] One article frames "trainer vs solver" as distinct products for different learners (beginner-oriented trainers versus solver-grade drill tools). — [PokerSkill blog](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/trainer-vs-solver/)

### Inferences
- The owner's "Train" mode differs from every product found: mainstream trainers quiz the *action for one hand* and grade it against solver output, whereas the tool quizzes *range-level concepts* (range advantage, nut advantage, largest bet the range takes). No mainstream product surfaced in this search drills those concepts directly; the nearest equivalents are aggregate reports (browse, not quiz). This is a differentiator, and it also means there is no industry-standard scoring to copy for concept questions.
- For any hand-action quiz, pure EV-loss grading is the most defensible on indifferent hands (it never punishes a choice the equilibrium is indifferent to) but teaches nothing about frequencies; frequency credit teaches the mix but produces the "same EV, different label" complaint. GTO Wizard's answer is to offer both plus a frequency-input mode and an RNG roll. A small tool could show both numbers side by side: "cost: 0.0% pot; solver takes this 30% of the time".
- Tier labels (best/inaccuracy/mistake/blunder) are borrowed from chess game review and are now a de facto convention; learners arriving from other tools will expect them.
- The DTO complaint (no explanation of why an action was wrong) points at the gap the owner's "Study" mode already fills: explanation of why a hand and a range act as they do. Linking a missed Train question straight into the Study explanation is a design few competitors appear to have.

### Gaps
- No sources found in this session for Odin, Deepsolver, Flopzilla/Equilab equity drills, Run It Once or Upswing training tools, or GTO+ / PioSOLVER trainer workflows. Their features must not be asserted from these notes.
- GTO Wizard's "analyze" mode, "spot the leak" and drill presets were not documented by any source reached; exact GTOW Score formula and tier cutoffs other than the 3.5% inaccuracy rule were not found.
- DTO Poker's grading rule for mixed strategies was not found.
- No independent or peer-reviewed study of whether any poker trainer improves real results was found. All effectiveness claims for poker tools are vendor or practitioner statements.

## 2. What do respected coaches and authors say about studying solver output effectively? (practitioner opinion throughout)

### Takeaway
The consistent practitioner message is: use solver output to learn why, compress it into a few usable heuristics by texture and range, and test understanding rather than memorise hand-by-hand frequencies. All of it is opinion; none of it has been tested experimentally as far as this search found.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Andrew Brokos frames GTO as "a way of thinking rather than a way of playing" and uses solvers to highlight key heuristics and show practical application of theory. — [Poker.org interview](https://www.poker.org/latest-news/interview-andrew-brokos-talks-about-gto-and-his-play-optimal-poker-books-ax5tx5K4xZ1I/)
- [PRACTITIONER, review of Brokos] *Play Optimal Poker* teaches through toy games (two or three hands, one street) small enough to work by hand, so the mechanics show through; reviewers call this its strongest teaching move and say the aim is reading solver output, not answers for specific hands. Reviews also caution that solver numbers change with ranges, bet sizes and the modelled game, so specific frequencies are not universal. — [OnlinePokerStrategy review](https://www.onlinepokerstrategy.com/poker-books/play-optimal-poker/); [PokerNews review](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2019/06/pokernews-book-review-play-optimal-poker-by-andrew-brokos-34596.htm)
- [PRODUCT/PRACTITIONER] GTO Wizard's blog recommends solving toy games by hand to learn the basic incentives of optimal play. — [GTO Wizard blog: How to solve toy games](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-solve-toy-games/)
- [PRACTITIONER, reviews of Acevedo] *Modern Poker Theory* is described as using solver output to understand the logic of strategies and then practising application; readers recommend running one's own hands through a solver and using the book to understand why; one reader stresses that theory matters only insofar as it produces strong heuristics for real games, and reports the author describes his own time as about 80% study, 20% play. The book is called dense and technical. — [Goodreads reader reviews](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/37493292-modern-poker-theory); [SmartPokerPro review](https://smartpokerpro.com/modern-poker-theory/)
- [PRODUCT/PRACTITIONER] GTO Wizard's "The Science of Learning Applied to GTO Wizard" applies Bjork's storage-strength versus retrieval-strength idea, retrieval practice ("recalling facts and concepts to strengthen long-term memory"), spacing ("the greater the drop in retrieval strength, the greater the increase in learning when the memory is accessed again") and mixing practice types so that practice is "closer to the real thing". — [GTO Wizard blog](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-science-of-learning-applied-to-gto-wizard/)
- [PRODUCT/PRACTITIONER] Aggregate reports are presented as the way to "build heuristics based on flop textures": categorise flops, look for common patterns, use the flop-grouping tool. — [GTO Wizard blog: Flop Heuristics, IP C-Betting in Cash Games](https://blog.gtowizard.com/flop-heuristics-ip-c-betting-in-cash-games/); [GTO Wizard blog: Mastering PKOs With Custom Aggregated Reports](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mastering-pkos-with-custom-aggregated-reports/)
- [PRACTITIONER] MTT Poker School's method: group the 1,755 flops, read the group trend, then open the sim only where one flop departs from its group. — [MTT Poker School OTB #005](https://www.mttpokerschool.com/single-post/otb-005-how-to-quickly-identify-the-heuristics-using-aggregate-reports)
- [PRACTITIONER, third-party digest of a GTO Wizard video] For multiway pots: build aggregate reports on a subset of 25-49 flops rather than all flops, extract heuristics, understand the strategy behind the numbers. — [Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/the-hidden-ev-in-3-way-pots-nobody-studies-this-by-gtowizard)
- [PRACTITIONER] Node-locking advice: state the study goal before each session; changing one node shifts earlier nodes unless previous streets are locked; use it to see how the solver adjusts on earlier and later streets when an opponent errs (for example, an opponent with no bluffs). — [GTO Wizard blog: How Pros Use Solvers To Crush Pool Leaks](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how_pros_use_solvers_to_crush_pool_leaks/); [GTO Wizard blog: How To Adjust When Villain Has No Bluffs](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-adjust-when-villain-has-no-bluffs/); [GTO Wizard blog: Adjusting on Later Streets After Exploiting Flop](https://blog.gtowizard.com/adjusting-on-later-streets-after-exploiting-flop/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Investigate solutions that look wrong to you rather than only checking whether your play matched. — [GTO Wizard blog: Hidden Gems for 2025](https://blog.gtowizard.com/unwrapping-gto-wizards-hidden-gems-for-2025/)
- [PRACTITIONER] A study guide recommends turning solver output into short table-usable rules, three to five per session, arguing that rules recallable under fatigue beat charts that cannot be retrieved in real time. — [VIP-Grinders: GTO and solvers](https://www.vip-grinders.com/poker-strategy/gto-and-solvers/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Phil Galfond's essay "A Pile of Non-Mistakes" surfaced as relevant to how to think about small EV losses versus real errors; content not read. — [philgalfond.com](https://www.philgalfond.com/articles/a-pile-of-non-mistakes/)

### Inferences
- The owner's Study mode ("why this hand acts so, why the range acts so, what would change that") is squarely the practice coaches recommend: explanation over memorisation, plus a counterfactual, which is the pedagogical purpose of node-locking without the tooling burden.
- Options suggested by this body of advice: (a) a texture-family view or quiz (aggregate-report style: "on ace-high dry boards the range bets small often; which of these four boards breaks the pattern?"); (b) a "what would change it" drill, where the learner predicts the direction of the strategy shift after a stated change (opponent never bluffs, stacks deeper, board pairs); (c) toy-game lessons that can be worked by hand on a phone; (d) a "name the heuristic" step at the end of a Study walk, limited to a few rules per session; (e) a "surprise me" queue of spots where the solution contradicts a common rule.
- "Outlier within a group" is a recurring practitioner technique and maps naturally to an error-driven prompt: show the rule, then the exception, and ask why.

### Gaps
- Nothing was found in this session from Matthew Janda, Will Tipton, Qing Yang (Finding Equilibrium), Tombos21 by name, or Daniel Dvoress / "Daily Dose of GTO". Their views are not represented and should not be attributed.
- Acevedo's and Brokos's own text was not read; findings are from reviews and one interview.
- "Simplification of strategies" (fewer bet sizes, pure rather than mixed play at small EV cost) is widely discussed among coaches but no source quantifying the EV cost of simplification was reached.
- No experimental evidence compares heuristic-first study with chart memorisation for poker.

## 3. Chess and other skill-app analogues, and the evidence or informed criticism of how well they teach

### Takeaway
Chess offers the closest designs: spaced-repetition line trainers, rated puzzles where learner and item both carry a rating, timed streak modes, and post-game review with graded moves. The best evidence (a 2026 Psychological Science study of 44,213 players) is that structured study activities are associated with far more rating gain per unit of effort than simply playing, yet players spend about 90% of their time playing; the commonest informed criticism is that move-recall trainers reward memorising lines over understanding. Duolingo's efficacy evidence is mostly company-funded, and simulation research shows debriefing matters more than its particular format.

### Cited Findings

**Chess: evidence**
- [PEER] Southwick, Harwell, Wright, Olsen and Ogles (2026), *Psychological Science*, "Not All Practice Is Created Equal: Longitudinal Evidence From Over 40,000 Chess Players": 44,213 Chess.com players, time-stamped practice and rating data over six months, comparing gameplay, game reviews, lessons and puzzles. Deliberate-practice-aligned activities showed about a 3.61-fold advantage in learning efficiency over gameplay, with meaningful differences between activities; players nonetheless spent roughly 90% of their time playing; effects were most modest for advanced players. The authors caution that separating practice from ability and other traits remains unresolved (association, not proven cause). — [DOI 10.1177/09567976261452568](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261452568); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42335356/)
- [COMMUNITY] A Lichess analysis of about 24,000 players found puzzle rating tracks game rating, and its author states this cannot show causation. — [Lichess blog: Do puzzles matter?](https://lichess.org/@/ggSayItBack/blog/do-puzzles-matter-a-data-analysis/twAyjQDX)
- [COMMUNITY, vendor-run] A "Woodpecker method" (repeat the same puzzle set in cycles) dataset of about 1,950 users and 432,000 puzzle attempts over 20 weeks reported rating gains, smaller above 1600, with the authors flagging self-selection and no control for other study. — [Lichess blog by Disco Chess](https://lichess.org/@/DiscoChessApp/blog/woodpecker-method-training-431000-puzzles-60000-rated-games-and-35-elo/yrWsFF1N)
- [COMMUNITY] One adult improver who gained about 600 points credited fast pattern-recognition practice rather than long calculation puzzles. — [Marcus Buffett newsletter](https://buttondown.com/mbuffett/archive/thoughts-on-chess-improvement-after-gaining-600/)

**Chess: designs**
- [PRODUCT] Lichess treats each puzzle attempt as a Glicko-2 game between player and puzzle, so both carry a rating and a rating deviation; the puzzle's rating is hidden until solved; choosing a theme gives fewer rating points because the theme is a hint; Puzzle Storm is a separate timed high-score mode. — [Lichess puzzle dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Lichess/chess-puzzles); [Lichess forum](https://lichess.org/forum/general-chess-discussion/puzzle-rating-question)
- [PRODUCT] Chess.com Game Review classifies moves with an Expected Points Model (win chance from engine evaluation and the player's rating); the label depends on expected points lost. Snippet of the official table (garbled, treat as approximate): Excellent about 0.00-0.02, Good 0.02-0.05, Inaccuracy 0.05-0.10, Mistake 0.10-0.20, Blunder 0.20+. "Great" marks the only good move in a position; "Brilliant" a sound sacrifice that is best or near-best; "Miss" a failure to punish an opponent's error. Chess.com says it is more generous with Brilliant and Great labels for lower-rated players. — [Chess.com support: how moves are classified](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8572705-how-are-moves-classified-what-is-a-blunder-or-brilliant-and-etc); [Chess.com: Game Review](https://www.chess.com/terms/game-review)

**Chess: informed criticism of spaced-repetition line trainers (Chessable MoveTrainer)**
- [PRACTITIONER] Andy Matuschak (researcher on spaced-repetition systems): MoveTrainer's prompts are move-based, so "there's no way to use this spaced repetition mechanism to reinforce conceptual knowledge". — [Andy Matuschak notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z8MgFD7B7QRqqGZXsJqeGpm5PJhroPwb6BW2V)
- [COMMUNITY] Users report the tool rewards memorising specific lines over understanding structures, and that lines are forgotten because the underlying ideas are weak; one writer says they were "tempted by quantity > quality"; another critic says opening trainers confuse "doing something once" with "knowing it"; common lines and never-seen engine lines are weighted equally. — [Chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/chess-openings/learning-with-movetrainer-vs-a-video-series); [Jun Taikeda Substack](https://juntaikeda.substack.com/p/3-lifetime-repertoires-the-10-pitfalls); [SmithyQ blog](https://smithyq.blogspot.com/2024/01/why-i-stopped-using-online-opening.html)
- [COMMUNITY] Defenders say scheduling is the main value and that memorisation and understanding are complementary; advice is to understand the idea on first study, then use repetition to retain it, and to be selective about what enters the queue. — [Zwischenzug Substack](https://zwischenzug.substack.com/p/how-to-use-chessable); [Lichess blog: how to use Chessable](https://bn.lichess.org/@/mainsequence/blog/how-to-use-chessable-when-studying-openings/o1tKSyfA)
- No controlled study of MoveTrainer's effect on understanding or rating was found; the criticism is anecdotal. — [Andy Matuschak notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zDr94hP6bG3jJYrdYy8B5hx)

**Duolingo**
- [PRODUCT, company research] Duolingo's Efficacy Research Lab reports gains in all four skills for Spanish-learning university students after three months, and mixed results in a Colombian study (gains for general learners, not for university English students). — [Duolingo blog: efficacy studies](https://blog.duolingo.com/results-duolingo-efficacy-studies)
- [Company-commissioned] The 2012 Vesselinov and Grego study was commissioned by Duolingo with data collection and analysis described as independent; it found the app somewhat more effective for absolute beginners than advanced learners. — [TechCrunch 2013](https://techcrunch.com/2013/01/17/study-learning-spanish-with-duolingo-can-be-more-effective-than-college-classes-or-rosetta-stone/amp)
- [PEER] Loewen et al. (2019), *ReCALL*: independent research on Duolingo has been limited and has raised concerns about persistence, motivation and efficacy; its review cites a study finding no difference in standardised test scores between app users and comparison learners. — [ReCALL case study](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A4D7C8F71782A37D258C19F357DDBCBE/S0958344019000065a.pdf/mobileassisted_language_learning_a_duolingo_case_study.pdf)
- [PEER] A 2024 *Language Learning & Technology* study followed 48 independent learners for three months; its authors note there is no published meta-analysis specifically of app-based language learning. — [LLT journal](https://www.lltjournal.org/item-detail/1152/)
- [Preprint, qualitative] "When Gamification Spoils Your Learning" analyses user forum posts about how Duolingo's gamification can work against learning (gamification misuse). — [arXiv 2203.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)
- [Student thesis] Mixed findings on Duolingo's game elements: some users said they raised engagement, others that they made learning less enjoyable. — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1866808/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

**Simulation and debriefing**
- [PEER] Cheng et al. (2014), *Medical Education*, systematic review and meta-analysis of debriefing in technology-enhanced simulation: 177 studies, 11,511 learners; simulation with debriefing beat no intervention on all outcomes (effect sizes 0.28 to 2.16); video-assisted debriefing showed negligible, non-significant advantage over non-video debriefing; other design features gave mixed or non-significant results. — [QxMD record](https://read.qxmd.com/read/24909527/debriefing-for-technology-enhanced-simulation-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis)
- [PEER] A 2020 *Nurse Education Today* meta-analysis (18 studies reviewed, 7 pooled) found an overall effect of 0.31 for structured debriefing, no significant difference between debriefing methods, and "insufficient evidence to identify the debriefing methods that are most effective". — [PubMed 32135455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32135455/)

### Inferences
- The Psychological Science result is the strongest available support for the general proposition behind a study tool: deliberate, feedback-rich activities beat mere play per unit of effort, and people under-use them. It is correlational, in chess, and on one platform; it says nothing direct about poker.
- Matuschak's criticism applies directly to any "review missed questions" queue: if the review re-asks the same board, the learner can memorise the answer to that board. Re-asking the *concept* on a fresh board with the same feature (the tool can generate boards) avoids this and doubles as a transfer test. This is an option competitors with fixed libraries cannot easily offer.
- The Lichess two-sided rating (item difficulty and learner skill estimated together) is a ready model for adaptive difficulty and for a progress number that is not a streak. Its "theme chosen means fewer points" rule is a neat way to handle hints.
- Chess.com's expected-points approach is the chess parallel of EV-loss grading: label by cost, not by deviation from the single top move. Its rating-adjusted generosity is an example of deliberately tuning feedback for motivation, which is a design choice the owner should make consciously.
- Debriefing research suggests the review step is where learning is consolidated, and that elaborate formats add little over a simple structured one. For a phone tool, a short structured after-session review (what you missed, why, one rule to carry) is likely enough.
- Duolingo's record is a caution: strong engagement design with a thin independent efficacy base.

### Gaps
- No sources reached for Aimchess, DecodeChess, Chess.com Puzzle Rush or "guess the move" training specifically, nor for critiques of AI move explanations.
- The lesson-versus-puzzle-versus-review breakdown inside the 2026 Psychological Science paper was not available from snippets; the full paper should be read before citing activity-specific numbers.
- Flight-simulator training transfer literature was not searched. The often-quoted Tannenbaum and Cerasoli (2013) finding that debriefs improve performance by roughly 20-25% was not confirmed in this session.
- Claims circulating in blogs that Duolingo streak features raised daily active users by a specific percentage, or that a randomised trial showed no advantage over other self-study, could not be traced to a primary source and are excluded.

## 4. Motivation, habit and engagement research relevant to a phone-based self-study app

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed meta-analyses find gamification gives small positive effects on learning (g about 0.5 cognitive, 0.36 motivational, 0.25 behavioural) with the motivational and behavioural effects unstable under rigorous designs; monitoring one's own progress reliably helps goal attainment; and streaks do drive continued behaviour, but a visibly broken streak reduces it, an effect softened when the streak can be repaired. Evidence that engagement mechanics can displace learning is real but mostly qualitative.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER] Sailer and Homner (2020, online 2019), *Educational Psychology Review* 32, 77-112, meta-analysis of gamification of learning: cognitive outcomes g = .49 (95% CI .30 to .69, k = 19, N = 1,686); motivational g = .36 (CI .18 to .54, k = 16, N = 2,246); behavioural g = .25 (CI .04 to .46, k = 9, N = 951). Only the cognitive effect was stable among high-rigor studies. Game fiction and social interaction moderated behavioural outcomes; competition combined with collaboration looked favourable. The authors conclude gamification works but "factors contributing to successful gamification are still somewhat unresolved". — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w); [open-access copy, Augsburg](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056)
- [PEER] A 2023 meta-analysis in *Educational Technology Research and Development* focused on intrinsic motivation and basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness): 35 independent interventions, about 2,500 participants, overall small effect favouring gamified learning (Hedges' g = 0.257). It maps mechanics to needs on theoretical grounds (leaderboards to competence, team competition to relatedness, choice of badges to autonomy), citing Sailer et al. 2017; this mapping is rationale, not a tested per-element effect. — [Springer, ETR&D 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)
- [PEER] Harkin et al. (2016), *Psychological Bulletin*, meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies (N = 19,951): interventions that increase monitoring of goal progress improve goal attainment, with the increase in monitoring mediating the effect; effects were larger when outcomes were reported or made public and when the information was physically recorded. — [White Rose repository](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/); [PubMed 26479070](https://www.pubmed.gov/26479070/)
- [PEER] Silverman and Barasch (2023), *Journal of Consumer Research* 49(6), 1095-1117, seven studies: people shown an intact logged streak are more likely to continue the behaviour than people shown a broken one, independent of actual past behaviour (it depends on how the log represents it); maintaining a logged streak becomes a goal in itself; the drop after a break is larger when the person blames themselves and smaller when the streak can be "repaired". — [University of Delaware repository](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb); [CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)
- [Secondary coverage of the same paper] Participants would take costly actions to repair a broken streak; Silverman suggests apps avoid dwelling on broken streaks and offer alternate ways to keep them going. A reported 59% of participants said they had gone out of their way to keep an app streak (figure from secondary coverage). — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation); [University of Delaware news](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman)
- [Secondhand citation of peer-reviewed work] Lally et al.'s habit-formation research is quoted as finding that "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour" did not materially affect habit formation. — [Yu-kai Chou blog on streak design](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/streak-design-gamification-motivation-burnout/)
- [PEER, contested] Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), *Psychological Bulletin* 125, 627-668: meta-analysis reporting that expected tangible rewards undermine intrinsic motivation; verbal rewards did not have that negative effect and unexpected tangible rewards showed no post-reward effect. Cameron and Pierce (1994) had concluded negative effects were limited; the dispute was never fully settled, and field studies of incentives generally find no long-term harm. — [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/short-term-rewards-dont-sap-long-term-motivation); [Review of Educational Research record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071001001)
- [Preprint, qualitative] Duolingo users describe gamification being "misused" in ways that work against learning (chasing points rather than studying). — [arXiv 2203.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)
- [Preprint, claim not finding] A 2026 paper on flashcard interaction design states that presence-based rewards may produce superficial engagement rather than deep learning. — [arXiv 2607.25096](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25096)
- [COMMUNITY/industry] A design survey of 859 streak implementations argues streaks work chiefly through fear of loss. — [Mobbin blog](https://mobbin.com/blog/streaks-are-about-losing-a-study-of-859-streak-designs)

### Inferences
- A daily score and a missed-question review, which the tool already has, are forms of progress monitoring, the mechanic with the strongest randomised evidence here (Harkin et al.). Making the record visible over time (a trend, not just today's number) is consistent with that evidence.
- If a streak is added, the Silverman and Barasch findings argue for a forgiving design: count days practised per week rather than an unbroken chain, allow repair, and never present a broken streak as a loss. Rewards tied to mere attendance are the kind most at risk of crowding out interest; informational feedback about competence (you now lose less EV on paired boards) is the kind least at risk.
- Self-determination theory suggests mapping features to needs: autonomy (learner chooses spot, texture family, session length; Study mode is inherently self-directed), competence (difficulty matched to skill, feedback that explains), relatedness (weakest for a solo phone tool; sharing a spot or comparing against an anonymous population benchmark are low-cost options). The meta-analytic effect on need satisfaction is small, so these are reasons to prefer one design over another, not promises of retention.
- Small, unstable behavioural effects of gamification mean points, badges and leaderboards should not be expected to carry retention by themselves. Sailer and Homner's stable effect is on cognitive outcomes, which fits gamification that is bound to the learning task (quiz feedback) rather than bolted on.
- The engagement-versus-learning risk is best controlled by choosing the tool's headline metric to be a learning measure (see section 5) rather than minutes, sessions or streak length.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session and therefore unsourced: Locke and Latham goal-setting findings (specific, difficult goals with feedback); mastery versus performance goal meta-analyses; Hanus and Fox (2015) on badges and leaderboards lowering motivation over a semester; Lally et al. (2010) primary text; per-need effect sizes in the 2023 ETR&D meta-analysis. These are well-known references worth checking directly but must not be cited from these notes.
- No research was found on adult self-directed learners of poker or gambling-adjacent skills specifically, nor on whether engagement mechanics in a gambling-related study app raise distinct concerns.
- No quantitative study isolating "time on app" optimisation harming learning outcomes was found; the evidence is qualitative.

## 5. Measurement: how could a tool show the learner real progress?

### Takeaway
Existing tools already expose the raw ingredients (EV loss per decision in big blinds or percent of pot, tiered error counts, frequency deviation, and Elo or Glicko-style skill ratings); none found in this search reports calibration, delayed retention or transfer to unseen spots, which are the measures that would separate learning from familiarity with a question bank.

### Cited Findings
- [PRODUCT] GTO Wizard reports GTOW Score, total EV loss in big blinds, average EV loss per hand, EV loss as percent of pot, and frequency difference. — [GTO Wizard help: Measure Performance](https://help.gtowizard.com/measure-performance/)
- [PRODUCT] Lucid Poker gives a 0-100 session grade, a rating breakdown and the single costliest error. — [Lucid Poker](https://lucidpoker.com/)
- [PRODUCT] Postflop+ keeps an Elo-style rating starting at 1200 that moves with decision difficulty and accuracy. — [Postflop+](https://thinkgto.com/apps/postflop-plus)
- [PRODUCT] Lichess rates player and puzzle jointly with Glicko-2, including a rating deviation (an uncertainty measure) for each. — [Lichess puzzle dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Lichess/chess-puzzles)
- [PEER] Monitoring progress improves goal attainment, more so when recorded and when reported. — [Harkin et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/)
- [PRODUCT/PRACTITIONER] Spaced retrieval is recommended for poker study on the basis that recalling after a drop in retrieval strength produces more learning. — [GTO Wizard blog: Science of Learning](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-science-of-learning-applied-to-gto-wizard/)
- [PEER] In chess, rating change over six months was the outcome used to compare practice activities, an example of measuring by an external performance criterion rather than in-app activity. — [Southwick et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261452568)
- [COMMUNITY] Puzzle rating correlates with game rating but the relation is not shown to be causal, a warning against treating an in-app rating as proof of real-game skill. — [Lichess blog](https://lichess.org/@/ggSayItBack/blog/do-puzzles-matter-a-data-analysis/twAyjQDX)

### Inferences
Options for the owner, none of which was found implemented in a competing product in this search:
- **Cost-based accuracy.** For action questions, average EV loss per decision as percent of pot, trended by week. For the concept questions (range advantage, nut advantage, largest bet size), an error-magnitude score: how far the answer was from the solved value, not just right or wrong.
- **Per-concept and per-texture skill map.** Separate estimates for each question type crossed with board family (paired, monotone, ace-high dry, low connected). This is the visible-progress device and directs what to study next.
- **Fresh-board transfer score.** Because the tool solves spots in the browser, every quiz board can be new. Reporting accuracy only on never-seen boards gives a transfer measure for free and answers the memorisation criticism levelled at Chessable.
- **Delayed retention checks.** Re-test a missed concept after days, on a different board with the same feature, and report "retained after a week" separately from same-day accuracy.
- **Calibration.** Ask for a confidence level before revealing the answer and show whether 80%-confident answers are right about 80% of the time. No poker or chess product found does this.
- **Two-sided rating.** A Glicko-style rating for the learner and for each question type gives adaptive difficulty and an honest uncertainty band.
- **Keep engagement numbers secondary.** Days practised and questions answered are worth showing as context, not as the headline.

### Gaps
- No published validation links any in-app poker metric (GTOW Score, EV loss in trainer) to real-money results.
- No source was found on calibration training or confidence-weighted testing in poker or chess tools; the calibration option rests on general reasoning, not on cited evidence.
- Solver EV differences below convergence noise are a known limit (noted by DriveGTO's manual); the tool's own solver accuracy would set the floor on how fine an EV-loss score can meaningfully be, and that floor was not researched here.
