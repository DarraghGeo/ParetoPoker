The ladder · both ranges as the hand unfolds

Two action bars per decision: out of position on top, in position below

Each bar is a range sorted weakest (left) to strongest (right). The player to act is coloured by what each part of the range does: passive on top, the most aggressive action at the bottom. The other bar is the range he is up against. Bars get shorter as hands leave. The white line is the value or price line. Tap an action to walk the line.

Which line follows the betting?

The bar is the range of the player to act, weakest on the left. Red from the bottom is how often that part bets. Each line is one measure of the hands in that part, drawn low to high on its own scale. A line that tracks the action rises where the red rises.

weakeststrongest

The numbers are the correlation between each line and how often the slice bets, in the weaker half and the stronger half of this one range: +1 rises and falls with the betting, 0 unrelated, −1 opposite. The first spot is the clearest one found; the other two are simply the next two in the data.

3 · Betting on the flop

The map: strength, and how much the hand still changes

On the flop and turn, betting does not fall in bands by strength. A second question sorts it: is the hand settled, or will the next card move it a lot?

fill = how often it bets

These are mock-ups on real solutions, not finished designs. The strips are cut into 40 equal slices of the range; the map uses fixed cuts (70% and 40% equity; more or less movement than the middle hand). In the experiments the line carried 69 to 88% of what hands do facing a bet, three bands carried 74% of river betting, and a six-region map carried 66% of flop betting when its cuts were fitted to each spot; with the fixed cuts drawn here this flop reaches 46%. No picture tested implied bet size.
