You bet a bit over a third of your range and check the rest.

He has checked twice, the second time after the third diamond came, so he has no flushes left. The very best hands are all yours. That is what lets you bet.

It is not that your range is ahead: overall the two are about even, and he has slightly more hands that are simply good. That is why most of your range still checks.

The bets come from both ends of the bar: flushes, sets and two pair on the right, and hands with nothing but a draw on the left. The pairs in the middle check.

Same spot, seen as a quiz question. You are BB; SB has checked the flop and the turn.
Who has more of the very best hands?
How sure are you?
8%of your range beats 90% of his
0%of his beats 90% of yours

You do. A third diamond came on the turn and he checked again. His flushes would have bet, so he has none left; yours are all still there.

Careful with the other question: on overall equity the ranges are about even (49% to 51%). Having the best hands and being ahead on average are different things, and they lead to different plans.
