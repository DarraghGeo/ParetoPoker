Pareto · design review

The butterfly chart: what to keep

This is the chart we built: both ranges back to back, sorted by what each hand has made, yours on the left and his on the right. Below it is what the testing since says about each of its layers, and then the same chart reworked on the same solved hand.

As built

Layer by layer

Keep

Hand-strength rows, two sides

Three well-placed strength bands held as much of a strategy as the whole 13×13 grid (0.7 and 0.8% of the pot lost, against 0.8 and 0.9). Players already think in these rows. This is the part of the chart that works.

Keep

Draws as their own row

Splitting hands by whether they have a draw cut the turn loss from 0.69 to 0.43%. It does nothing on the river, where the row disappears anyway.

Keep

Colour by the solver's action

Shows the shape at a glance. Playing each hand's commonest action lost nothing, so two colours per bar are enough; finer mixes are not worth showing.

Change

The line counted down his side

Reading a threshold by counting his bars cost about 1.3 points of the pot over a full hand (3.2% against 1.9% with true equity). A row is not an equity: your top pair and his sit in the same row. Put each row's equity on the chart and draw the line where it is exact.

Change

The thin advantage lines

Range advantage by top pair or better is sound (right on 96 to 99% of flops where the gap is real) and it drives the flop. But it is one comparison, and two extra bars are a heavy way to show it. Say it in a line of text. Nut advantage only mattered for river sizing; fold it into the same line.

Drop

The white line through each bar

Which exact hand inside a row bets was worth 0.3 to 0.4% in total, and blockers 0.1 to 0.2%. The line's link to the action was real but loose (0.3 to 0.5), and it is the hardest part of the chart to read.

Proposed

The same butterfly, stripped down. The rows are short codes and the two sides sit close together, so the shape of both ranges reads at a glance. No numbers on the chart at all. A threshold is drawn only where it is exact: the price of a call. The numbers are one tap away: a row opens to show its share of each range and its hands, each with its equity.

TR+ trips or better · 2P two pair · OP overpair · TP top pair · MP middle pair · LP low pair · DR draws · Air nothing

What I need from you
  1. Is this the right amount on the rows: a code and two bars, nothing else?
  2. Are you happy to lose the white lines and the two thin bars?
  3. When betting, should the chart draw no line (as here), or draw our rule's line and mark it as a rule, not the solver's?

Both charts are drawn from one solved hand: single-raised pot, button against big blind, K♠ 9♦ 4♠ 7♥ then 2♣. Every percentage on them is from that solve.
